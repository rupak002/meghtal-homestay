Our Story

Three Generations, One Hillside

From a two-room cottage in the 1970s to a six-room homestay — still run by the same family.

The Beginning

Built By Hand, Room By Room

Meghtal began as a two-room cottage built by our grandfather in the 1970s. Today it's six rooms, a terraced vegetable garden, and a kitchen that still runs on a wood-fired stove for the momos and gundruk soup we serve at breakfast.

We keep the homestay small on purpose — enough rooms to know every guest by name, and enough silence left on the hillside for you to actually hear it.

What We Stand For

How We Host

A few things that haven't changed since the first guests stayed in 1978.

🌿

Grown Here, Cooked Here

Breakfast is whatever came from the garden that morning. The vegetable plot behind the cottage supplies most of what lands on your table.

🔥

A Lit Fire Every Evening

From October to March, the sitting-room fire is going by 6 PM. Bring a book and a blanket — the clouds usually roll in just after sunset.

🤗

Quiet By Design

No conference packages, no large groups. We cap the homestay at twelve guests so the hillside stays quiet enough to actually hear the birds.

🏔

Views, Not Walls

Every room faces the range. Windows are wide and positioned so the mountains are the first thing you see when you wake up.

👪

Family Run

You'll be looked after by the same family that built this place. There's no front desk — just a knock on the kitchen door.

♻

Light Footprint

Solar water heating, composting, and a ban on single-use plastic. The hillside looked after us first — we return the favour.

History

A Short Timeline

1978

The Cottage Is Built

Our grandfather completes the first two rooms. The roof is corrugated tin. No guests yet — just the family and the view.

1989

First Paying Guests

A group of trekkers ask to stay the night. They leave a small note in a notebook. That notebook is still in the sitting room.

2004

The Garden Bath Cottage Added

Our most-requested room is completed — the open-air bath tub looking into the forest is our father's idea.

2015

Solar Panels Installed

Hot water is now solar-heated year-round. The wood stove stays — for cooking, not for heating water.

Today

Six Rooms, Same Family

Third generation now runs the homestay. The view from the balcony is the same. The gundruk recipe has not changed.
